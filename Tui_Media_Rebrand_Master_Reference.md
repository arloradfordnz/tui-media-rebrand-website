# Tui Media Rebrand — Master Reference

*Last updated: 25 August 2026*

---

## 1. The Core Shift

Tui Media, based in Nelson, New Zealand, is moving away from retainer-based social media content and into one-off video ad projects.

- **Reference model:** Zipline Marketing (ziplinemarketing.com.au) — a done-for-you video ads business built on project pricing rather than retainers, with strategy, production, and media buying handled in-house.
- Retainers are being **dropped entirely**.
- **"Not an agency" positioning is central and non-negotiable.**
- Copy and pitch should avoid sounding like a direct copy of Zipline's own language, even though the underlying model and structure are closely modelled on theirs.

---

## 2. The Offer

Each project runs through a fixed process:

**Strategise → Script → Film → Edit → Launch & manage**

- The launch/manage phase runs the ad campaign for a set **one-month period**, priced into the single project fee (not billed as an ongoing service).
- At the end of that month, **everything is handed over to the client**: raw footage, final cuts, and the ad account itself.
- **No guarantees of results, no ongoing retainer, no lock-in.**
- Pricing will **not** be positioned as the cheapest option on the market.
- The business will **actively turn away poor-fit clients** rather than take on everyone who enquires.

---

## 3. Target Clients

The Nelson/Tasman region is small, so realistic target verticals are ones with genuine margin, a clear buyer, and often a national or export customer base rather than pure local foot traffic.

**Strong fits:**
- Construction
- Marine and engineering
- Agriculture/horticulture equipment
- Tourism operators

**Poor fits (filter out early):**
- Cafés
- Retail
- Anything without spare margin to fund paid ad spend

---

## 4. Skills & Learning Plan

The founder is confident in video production but is deliberately building marketing skills he doesn't yet have:

- Direct-response copywriting and hook-writing
- Meta ads media buying
- Basic offer diagnosis
- Enough funnel/landing-page sense to spot when a client's own website will undercut a campaign

**Approach to media buying:** learning solo rather than bringing in a freelancer, via:
1. Meta Blueprint (free courses)
2. Daily study of Meta Ad Library in target verticals
3. Running real ad budget on Tui Media's own account first
4. A handful of discounted case-study projects with known clients before pricing at full rate

---

## 5. Brand Identity

- **Name and logo are staying as-is** — the tui bird mark paired with the "TuiMedia" wordmark (bold-and-thin weights of the same typeface) is considered strong; no rebuild needed.
- A reversed white version of the logo needed both the bird and wordmark inverted together to work properly.
- **Typeface direction for the new site:** sharper, more grotesk (in the spirit of Inter or General Sans) rather than soft/rounded/app-like — to better match the confidence of the logo and the seriousness of the new positioning.

---

## 6. Voice & Copy Rules

- Plain and direct. Avoid "unneeded" marketing words used just to sound impressive.
- **No em dashes** in any writing produced for this project.
- Founder prefers **iterating together** on copy rather than receiving a single finished version outright.
- **"Storytelling"** is specifically rejected — the kind of inflated marketing language he dislikes.
- **"Video ads"** is the preferred term — not "video marketing" (the vague phrase the old retainer-era site used).
- Draft pitch language pulled from founder's own notes:
  - Not an agency
  - Zero lock-ins or commitments
  - No guarantees
  - Not the cheapest on the market
  - "We don't take everyone"
- Headline direction: name a concrete outcome (leads, enquiries, bookings) alongside "video ads."
- Agency-style language (e.g., listing services like "strategy, production, and optimisation") conflicts with the voice — avoid it.
- The "not an agency" framing is a meaningful differentiator and should be preserved where possible rather than dropped for brevity.

---

## 7. Website Restructure (tuimedia.nz)

Current site is built almost entirely around old monthly retainer language and needs a full rework.

**Proposed new section order:**
1. Hero (outcome-led headline)
2. Proof / client logos
3. "Who this is for / not for" (qualifying section)
4. Process (strategise, script, film, edit, launch & manage)
5. Case studies (real results, once available)
6. Pricing tiers (without necessarily showing exact numbers)
7. About / founder section
8. Rewritten FAQ
9. Application form (replaces simple "book a call" link)

Site metadata and SEO also need updating — currently targets retainer-style search terms.

---

## 8. Two-Brand Structure

Two fully separate brands, each with own website and Instagram account:

- **Tui Media** — commercial video ads business, funnel-driven and results-focused.
- **Personal brand** (separate) — creative work such as brand films and spec ads, aimed at showcasing range and craft rather than generating leads. Likely built around the founder's own name rather than a studio alias.
